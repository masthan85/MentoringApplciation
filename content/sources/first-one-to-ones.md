# Running useful one-to-ones as a new manager

A regular one-to-one is the person's time, not yours. Its purpose is to build trust, remove obstacles and help them grow.

## Set the pattern

Meet weekly or fortnightly for thirty to sixty minutes, and protect the slot. Keep a shared document where both of you add topics. Let them go first.

## Useful questions

What is going well, and what is getting in your way? What would you like to learn this quarter? Is there anything I should be doing differently? Where are you unclear on priorities?

## What to avoid

Turning it into a status update, which can be done in writing. Cancelling often, which says the person is low priority. Doing all the talking. Raising a serious issue for the first time in a performance review.

## Follow through

Write down what you each agreed to do and check it at the next meeting. Small promises kept build trust faster than big ones.

## Difficult matters

For conduct, grievance or capability issues, follow your organisation's process and involve HR early. In the UK, ACAS publishes practical guidance for managers and employees.
