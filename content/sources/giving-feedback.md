# Giving feedback that people can use

Good feedback is specific, timely and about behaviour and its effect, not about the person's character.

## A simple structure

Situation: when and where it happened. Behaviour: what you saw or heard, without judgement. Impact: the effect it had. Then ask for their view, and agree what to do next.

For example: "In yesterday's design review, you interrupted twice while Sam was explaining. I noticed Sam stopped contributing after that. What was going on for you?"

## Timing and place

Give feedback soon after the event, privately for corrective feedback and publicly for recognition when the person would welcome it. Do not save things up for a review.

## Positive feedback counts

Say what was good and why it mattered, specifically, so it can be repeated.

## Receiving feedback

Ask for it, listen without defending, thank the person and decide what you will do. If you manage others, how you receive feedback sets the tone for the team.
