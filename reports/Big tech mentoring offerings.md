# Big tech sells the plumbing, not the mentor

As of October 2026, none of AWS/Amazon, Microsoft/LinkedIn or Google sells an AI product that acts as an individual's long-term mentor, and none of the learning platforms or frontier labs surveyed does either. What exists falls into three groups. The first is catalogue-bound tutors and simulators (AWS Skill Builder, Microsoft's Learning Agent, LinkedIn Learning's AI Coaching, Google's Guided Learning and study notebooks, Coursera, Udemy, Pluralsight, DataCamp). The second is employer-licensed coaches (Workera Sage, Degreed Maestro, Multiverse Atlas, Hone). The third is general assistants with cross-chat memory (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot). Across roughly 60 search-based lookups I found no editable goal model, month-over-month progress evidence, chosen mentor persona or human handoff in any of them, but that is **absence of evidence from search summaries, not proof**. The hyperscalers are best understood as your suppliers rather than competitors: AWS (Bedrock, AgentCore), Microsoft (Foundry, Agent Framework) and Google (Vertex Agent Engine, ADK) all ship memory and agent primitives that a provider-agnostic app like yours can sit on, and each has a London region story, though each story has caveats. The nearest competitive feature sets are Hone (memory plus proactive check-ins, enterprise, US) and the free general assistants, which will keep absorbing commodity features. A credible difference is accountability, inspectable user-owned memory and honest challenge, aimed at UK data, AI and software professionals paying for themselves. The risks are real: effectiveness evidence is thin and mixed, sycophancy is documented, and commodity layers will be bundled free. **Almost nothing here was read at primary source.** Every web fetch failed with DNS errors, so the claims rest on search summaries, mirrors of Microsoft Message Center items, aggregators and vendor pages, and the closing section lists what to check first.

## How to read this evidence, and what it updates

All five research notes say the same thing about method: pages could not be fetched, so findings come from search-result summaries, many of them secondary (Message Center mirrors, price aggregators, vendor release notes, affiliate-style reviews). Only one primary page, the Azure interview-coach repository README, was read in full. I therefore treat every date, price and licensing statement below as a lead rather than a fact, and I say so where a claim rests on a single weak source. No GBP price was found for most products. The "gap" conclusions (nobody offers goal tracking, evidence-based progress, personas or human handoff) are inferences from not finding something, and the labs and hyperscalers ship monthly, so a feature could exist that the searches missed.

This builds on, and does not repeat, the earlier landscape report ([AI mentoring apps landscape 2026](AI%20mentoring%20apps%20landscape%202026.md)), which covered employer coaches such as Valence, BetterUp and CoachHub, the human marketplaces, the head-to-head coaching trial and the sycophancy study. Four of its statements are now updated. First, it said Microsoft 365 Copilot memory reached general availability in July 2026; the Message Center item (MC1158329) has since moved again, with one version showing a mid-September start and late-October finish, and the latest date is unconfirmed ([MC1158329](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1158329)). Second, it described Gemini Gems as holding only setup context and cited an old source; Google's UK blog now reports Gemini "Memories" live in the UK on 29 April 2026, on by default, with tools to import chat history from other AI apps ([Google UK blog](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/gemini-launches-new-personalisation-features-in-the-uk/)). Third, it listed LinkedIn's AI coach, Coursera Coach and Khanmigo as unsearched; they are covered below. Fourth, it called the EU AI Omnibus delay "reported"; law-firm commentary now gives the regulation number and says it entered into force on 27 July 2026, with the Annex III high-risk date moved to 2 December 2027 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/27/the-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force-today-102nedo)). Nothing found contradicts the earlier report's main conclusions.

## Hyperscalers ship content, simulators and building blocks, not mentors

**AWS and Amazon** have the deepest technical learning catalogue and the most AI features bolted onto it, all scoped to AWS skills. Reported 2025 to 2026 additions include Lab Maker, which generates personalised labs from a plain-language goal ([AWS Training and Certification blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/training-and-certification/?trk=sm); launch date unverified), a Meeting Simulator that lets learners rehearse presenting AI solutions to sceptical AI personas, with 220+ free AI courses ([MIT Sloan Management Review MENA](https://www.mitsloanme.com/article/aws-rolls-out-new-tools-to-build-job-ready-ai-skills/); secondary), and a relaunched Cloud Quest built around an AI customer conversation ([AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/training-and-certification/introducing-the-new-aws-cloud-quest-ai-powered-practice-hands-on-building-and-a-path-to-official-aws-badges/)). Individual Skill Builder pricing was launched at **$29 a month or $299 a year**, with AWS promotional terms suggesting a $449 regular annual rate ([AWS promotional terms](https://aws.amazon.com/training/skill-builder-promotional-terms-conditions/)); these figures are conflicting and possibly stale, and no GBP price was found. Amazon's UK Career Choice programme pre-pays up to 100 percent of tuition, capped at £3,000 a year, for employees ([Amazon UK](https://www.aboutamazon.co.uk/workplace/upskilling)); that is funding rather than coaching. Q Developer and Kiro are coding tools with no found coaching mode. Alexa+ launched in UK Early Access on 19 March 2026 with cross-conversation memory ([Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/e-innovation/artificial-intelligence/amazon-alexa-uk-629179)), but it is a home assistant. The Meeting Simulator's persona role-play is the one AWS pattern worth borrowing.

**Microsoft and LinkedIn** sell to employers. The Learning Agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot reportedly became generally available in June 2026 and replaced the Viva Learning Copilot experience, with rollout through late August ([Topedia](https://blog-en.topedia.com/2026/06/learning-agent-is-now-generally-available-in-microsoft-365-copilot/); a mirror of Message Center item MC1450135). Its skills assessments and role-based scenarios need an active Microsoft 365 Copilot licence ([handsontek](https://m365admin.handsontek.net/?p=23097)), which UK resellers list at roughly **£23 to £25 per user per month** on an annual term ([Syntax](https://www.syntax.co.uk/blog/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-costs-and-options-for-uk-businesses/); reseller figures, low confidence). A "Career Coach" agent appears among Microsoft's agent templates, but its general-availability status and licensing are unverified and one Microsoft support page frames it for students ([Microsoft agents](https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/microsoft-365-copilot/agents-built-by-microsoft)); the note's inference is that these are instruction templates, with no evidence of goal tracking. On LinkedIn, the Learning "AI Coaching" chatbot answers questions and recommends courses from the library ([LinkedIn Learning](https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/learner-engagement/linkedin-learning-ai-powered-coaching)), and role-play scenarios admins can assign were listed for September 2025 ([LinkedIn product updates](https://learning.linkedin.com/customer-success-center/product-updates/all-updates/q1-26)). Premium members reportedly got an AI career coach for job fit, company research and interview prep, in beta at the time of coverage ([Business Insider via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/linkedin-just-launched-ai-job-153328000.html)); a low-quality review says it is weaker than ChatGPT for open-ended coaching but knows your profile. LinkedIn holds the richest career data of any player here, yet nothing found suggests goal history or between-session follow-up. The old peer "Career Advice" mentoring feature launched in 2017 is reportedly discontinued, on a single third-party claim ([Hiration](https://hiration.com/blog/linkedin-career-advice)).

**Google** has no mentor product. Career Dreamer, a Labs experiment launched in February 2025, builds a "career identity" and a web of matching careers using Lightcast and BLS data; it was US-only at launch and I found no UK expansion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/19/googles-career-dreamer-uses-ai-to-help-you-explore-job-possibilities)). Gemini's Guided Learning (August 2025) is a free Socratic mode, and study notebooks (25 June 2026) add a diagnostic quiz, gap-based lessons and a progress dashboard, but they target students and exams ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-study-notebooks/)). That is the closest Google feature to adaptive tracking, and it shows the capability exists. Google Skills consolidated roughly 3,000 courses ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/10/14/google-skills-googles-ai-learning-platform/)), and Grow with Google's AI Essentials runs on Coursera behind a subscription after a trial, with the page returned quoting $49 a month and no verified GBP price ([Grow with Google UK](https://grow.google/intl/uk/enroll-certificates/ai-essentials-mid/)). Gemini Personal Intelligence, which links Gmail, Photos and Search, launched in US beta on 14 January 2026 for paid subscribers, and UK availability was not found ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/14/geminis-new-beta-feature-provides-proactive-responses-based-on-your-photos-emails-and-more)).

## The same gap appears in learning platforms and frontier labs, but the nearest rivals sit here

Learning platforms add catalogue-bound AI and sell the richest versions to employers. Coursera and Udemy agreed an all-stock merger in December 2025 worth about $2.5bn combined, with closing expected in the second half of 2026; whether it closed is unconfirmed ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251217155893/en/Coursera-to-Combine-with-Udemy-to-Empower-the-Global-Workforce-with-Skills-for-the-AI-Era)). Coursera Coach exists alongside Role Play, but no feature detail or price was found. Udemy expanded AI Role Play on 7 May 2026 as an enterprise add-on at undisclosed price ([Udemy Business](https://business.udemy.com/?p=99918)). Khanmigo costs a reported $4 a month but targets schools ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/khanmigo/pricing)). Codecademy and Boot.dev sit at roughly $20 to $60 a month, with sources in conflict ([Boot.dev comparison](https://www.boot.dev/blog/education/bootdev-vs-codecademy)).

The products closest to a mentor are enterprise-sold. Workera's Sage runs conversational skills assessments across domains and added voice and AI-specific assessments ([Training Industry](https://trainingindustry.com/?p=119101); vendor claims of 100M+ skills verified are unverified). Degreed Maestro offers AI voice and text coaching but nothing was found after April 2025, so its status is unverified ([Josh Bersin](https://joshbersin.com/2025/04/degreed-plots-a-comeback-with-major-ai-announcements/)). Multiverse's Atlas AI coach reportedly has 23,000 users and sits on a reported $70M raise, both from secondary sources ([Tamradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/multiverse-70m-ai-upskilling)). **Hone is the most worrying comparator**: its release notes say the AI Coach retained context across conversations from May 2026 and began proactive Slack and Teams check-ins in April 2026 ([Hone release notes](https://admin-support.honehq.com/hc/en-us/articles/32960544210701-Hone-Product-Release-Notes)). That is memory plus proactive follow-up, the combination I had earlier found nowhere, though it is vendor-reported, US-focused and aimed at managers and enterprise. It refines, rather than refutes, the earlier gap claim: the gap is narrower for enterprise and still open for self-funded individuals.

Frontier labs are the real everyday alternative. ChatGPT's study mode works on all plans and uses memory when on ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/articles/11780217)); Claude extended memory to Pro and Max on 23 October 2025 with per-project memory, view and edit controls and incognito chats ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory)); ChatGPT Pulse (proactive daily updates) launched in preview for Pro in September 2025 with no confirmed wider rollout found ([Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/a-i/openai-a-i/327299/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-in-preview)). No source shows structured goals, skill ratings or user-scheduled accountability loops in any of them, which is again absence of evidence against labs that ship monthly. Gemini's new chat-history import also means a user's history can move between assistants, which lowers switching costs for everyone including you.

## Hyperscalers are your suppliers, and the portability work is already done

For an app that is already provider-agnostic, the hyperscalers matter as infrastructure and distribution. Each offers the missing plumbing and none a mentoring blueprint. AWS pairs Strands agents with AgentCore Memory (short-term session and configurable long-term memory) ([aws-samples](https://github.com/aws-samples/sample-getting-started-with-amazon-agentcore)); Microsoft has Memory in Foundry Agent Service, announced at Ignite in November 2025 as a managed long-term store with item-level create, update and delete and retention controls, with consumption billing from 1 June 2026 ([Foundry blog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/foundry/introducing-memory-in-foundry-agent-service/); preview status in October unconfirmed); and Google has Vertex Agent Engine Sessions and Memory Bank, reported generally available from December 2025 with billing from 28 January 2026 ([Agent Builder release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/agent-builder/release-notes)). The best Microsoft reference is a read-in-full official sample, an interview coach built on Agent Framework, Foundry and Cosmos DB, whose README warns adopters to review authentication, per-user access and retention before using real data ([Azure-Samples](https://github.com/Azure-Samples/interview-coach-agent-framework)). Its saved interview records show the memory pattern, but goal and evidence tracking is left to the builder, as it is on AWS and Google.

UK residency is plausible on all three but never clean. AgentCore Memory is reported to support London, though only a subset of AgentCore features does ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/agentcore-regions.html)), and Claude Opus 4.6 on Bedrock lists London in-region inference while the Opus 4.5 card lists London only as Geo and Global routing ([Opus 4.6 card](https://docs.aws.eu/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-opus-4-6.html)). Vertex lists London for Runtime, Sessions and Memory Bank, but one overview page still labels Memory Bank preview, and no explicit residency guarantee was found ([Locations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/agent-builder/locations)). Microsoft announced in-country Copilot processing for the UK, though lower-quality sources claim Claude-generated output and peak-time routing can leave the EU boundary ([cloudswitched](https://www.cloudswitched.com/news/microsoft-365-copilot-anthropic-default-may-2026-uk-business-opt-in-guide); unconfirmed). Three practical implications follow. Do not market "UK data residency" as a blanket claim until you have verified the exact model and endpoint combination in your own account. Keep your memory and goal store in your own database, because hyperscaler memory services are young, their pricing recently changed to consumption billing, and their docs contradict themselves on status. And treat Copilot, Teams and Workspace as distribution surfaces and as competitors only if you sell through employers, since their coach features need employer licences.

## Where a UK-first mentor can be different, and what could sink it

The defensible difference is not memory, which every assistant now has, but what none of the surveyed products documents: a user-owned, inspectable and editable record of goals, skills and evidence; scheduled follow-up between sessions; a chosen mentor stance (the AWS persona simulator and Hone's proactive nudges both show the pattern works); honest challenge; and eventually referral to human mentors. UK specifics add weight: Career Dreamer and Alexa+ coaching are not UK products, Google's UK element is a 2022 employer consortium ([Computer Weekly](https://computerweekly.com/news/252521648/British-businesses-to-recognise-Google-skills-certificates)), and levy money flows to employers, not individuals. Government plans free AI training for 10 million workers by 2030 with Amazon, Google and Microsoft as partners ([gov.uk](https://gov.uk/government/news/free-ai-training-for-all-as-government-and-industry-programme-expands-to-provide-10-million-workers-with-key-ai-skills-by-2030)), which sets a free floor at foundation level; your product has to sit above it, on career navigation and accountability. The target segment is plausible but unproven: I found no UK willingness-to-pay data for data, AI and software professionals.

The effectiveness evidence supports accountability as the active ingredient but does not yet support a memory-enabled LLM mentor. A March 2026 preprint (N=517) found an LLM career coach beat no support on goal progress at two weeks (d=0.33) but not a written-reflection control, with the effect running through perceived accountability ([arXiv 2603.17887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17887); not peer reviewed, two-week horizon). The 2026 head-to-head trial reported effects only for human coaching ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.24384/5cgf-ab69); abstract-level). Unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores but cut later exam scores by 17 percent in a PNAS study of about 1,000 students, while a guardrailed tutor avoided the loss ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)). Most trials are on students, not working professionals, and no one has trialled a memory-enabled LLM mentor with them. A Science study found models affirm users 49 percent more than humans do, and users preferred the sycophantic ones ([Stanford News](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/03/ai-advice-sycophantic-models-research)), so retention incentives and honesty pull apart. A Microsoft and CMU survey of 319 knowledge workers linked higher confidence in generative AI to less critical thinking, though it measured self-report, not skill loss ([R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/microsoft-and-cmu-genai-may-undercut-critical-thinking-but-theres-an-antidote/)).

The commercial and regulatory risks are these. Commodity layers (Socratic mode, memory, import tools) will be bundled free, though I found no sourced case of a coaching startup displaced by a lab feature, so this is reasoned rather than documented. Capital is concentrated: OpenAI and Anthropic took about 80 percent of the $305.6bn raised by the 2026 Forbes AI 50 cohort ([MarketScale](https://marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/the-2026-forbes-ai-50-reveals-a-3056-billion-private-market-consolidating-fast-around-a-handful-of-giants)). Enterprise rivals such as Hone and Multiverse will push down the individual price while employer money stays elsewhere. On regulation, the UK Data (Use and Access) Act replaced Article 22 with a looser automated-decision regime reportedly commenced on 5 February 2026 ([Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/)), so a pure advisory mentor sits mostly under ordinary UK GDPR duties. The sharper exposures are special-category data that users volunteer in conversation, any employer-paid deployment where outputs reach HR or inform performance, and emotion inference. Ofcom's guidance puts standalone chatbots in the Online Safety Act only if they meet its service definitions, so adding community or peer features could bring you in scope ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/technology-digital/spring-2026/ofcom-publishes-an-explainer-on-the-regulation-of-ai-chatbots-under-the-online-safety-act/)). Enforcement so far targets companion chatbots and minors, such as Italy's EUR 5m Replika fine ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/ai-the-italian-supervisory-authority-fines-company-behind-chatbot-replika_el)) and Ofcom's investigation of the AI companion service Novi ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-investigates-ai-companion-chatbot-service)), so keeping scope to career and skill development, with crisis escalation, is prudent. OpenAI has acknowledged that safeguards can degrade in long conversations in the Raine litigation (allegations, not findings), which argues for session-level safety evaluation. This is research, not legal advice, and the notes themselves recommend UK data-protection counsel before launch.

## Conclusion

The sharper reading after this round is that the hyperscalers have effectively handed you the build-versus-buy answer: they sell memory, agent runtimes and UK regions, and they sell learning content to employers, so the competitive pressure on an individual-facing mentor comes from free general assistants and from enterprise coaches like Hone moving down-market, not from AWS, Microsoft or Google. That also shifts the moat. Neither memory nor adaptivity is scarce, and Google has already shown diagnostic, progress-tracked learning for students. What remains scarce is evidence that an AI mentor changes outcomes for working professionals and a relationship the user owns and can audit.

The practical implication is to treat the first UK pilot as an experiment on accountability and honesty, with a plain-assistant baseline, a disclosed outcome measure and a design that withholds answers rather than flatters. The earlier report's gap hypothesis survives, narrowed: open for self-funded UK individuals, closing for enterprise. It remains a hypothesis built on search summaries.

## Claims most worth verifying first

1. Hone's memory and proactive check-in features, price, and whether it sells to individuals or in the UK (release notes only, vendor-reported). This is the claim most likely to narrow your gap.
2. Whether any lab or hyperscaler now ships user-scheduled goal check-ins or an editable goal model: ChatGPT Pulse rollout beyond Pro, Gemini scheduled actions and Gems memory, Claude learning mode, Copilot memory GA status (the date has moved repeatedly).
3. LinkedIn Premium's AI career coach: UK availability, GBP price, data handling, and whether the 2017 Career Advice mentoring is truly discontinued (third-party claim). It is the only competitor with the career dataset.
4. Microsoft Learning Agent licensing and the Career Coach agent's status, from primary Message Center and Learn pages (all current detail is from mirrors).
5. UK residency claims for each stack: AgentCore Memory in London, Bedrock Claude in-region versus geo routing, Vertex Memory Bank in europe-west2 (docs contradict each other on preview versus GA), Foundry memory in UK South, with memory unit prices.
6. The Schimpf preprint and the de Haan, Terblanche and Nowack paper, read in full, including attrition; the Science sycophancy paper; and the UK Data (Use and Access) Act commencement dates and the ICO's final ADM guidance.
7. Whether the Coursera-Udemy merger closed, Coursera Coach's features and pricing, and GBP prices for AWS Skill Builder, Google AI Pro, Pluralsight and DataCamp.
8. Demand evidence the notes do not contain: UK data, AI and software professionals' willingness to pay, what they use today, and who pays at their employer.
