## Market leaders and product profiles

### Takeaway
The market splits into three models: AI-first enterprise coaches (Valence/Nadia), human-coach networks adding AI layers (BetterUp, CoachHub AIMY, Torch Spark, Ezra/LHH Cai), and cheap self-serve or creator-clone tools (Rocky.ai, Coachvox). Valence is the best-funded AI-native player; Pi is effectively a dormant consumer product. Independent evidence is thin, and most comparison pages are vendor-authored.

## Long-term memory, goal tracking, progress measurement

### Takeaway
Only Valence documents a named memory layer; others emphasise assessments, nudges and analytics dashboards. Details of memory implementation are mostly undisclosed.

### Cited Findings
- Valence describes a "proprietary memory and context engine" making coaching cumulative rather than episodic — [Valence trust and security](https://www.valence.co/trust-and-security); [Valence Nadia FAQ](https://support.valence.co/en/articles/9100961-valence-s-ai-coach-nadia-introduction-and-faqs)
- Valence aggregated reporting only for organisations with 25+ active users; retention limited to the enterprise contract life; leaders, HR and admins cannot access individual conversations — same sources
- CoachHub AIMY 2.0: dynamic self-assessments, Teams/email nudges, HRIS integrations and analytics dashboards — [CoachHub](https://www.coachhub.com/news-media/coachhub-launches-ai-coach-aimy-2)
- Torch: progress measured via 360 assessments tied to company leadership capacities — [Torch vs Ezra](https://torch.io/compare/ezra-vs-torch/)
- Rocky.ai: daily micro-sessions and goal tracking — [AI Tools Bakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/rocky-ai-review/)
- Valence sells the proven-outcomes angle via adoption stats (usage, NPS) rather than independent outcome studies (see single-case claim above) — [Risely](https://risely.me/compare/best-ai-coaching-platforms/)

### Inferences
- Progress measurement is mostly engagement metrics, self-assessments and 360s, not validated outcome studies.
- The privacy-vs-reporting trade-off (individual confidentiality, aggregated analytics) is a common enterprise design pattern.

### Gaps
- Memory architecture (summaries, vector stores, user-editable memory) for BetterUp, CoachHub, Rocky.ai, Pi: not found.
- Independent evidence that memory improves outcomes: not found.

## Safety boundaries and regulation positioning

### Takeaway
Vendors position as workplace coaching, not therapy, with topic guardrails and privacy promises; ISO 42001 is emerging as a differentiator. I found no explicit regulatory (EU AI Act, FDA, UK) positioning statements.

### Cited Findings
- Valence says Nadia is purpose-limited to coaching, with guardrails around hiring, termination, stereotypes, harm to self or others and criminal acts; the FAQ says Nadia is trained not to address Employee Relations, DEI issues, harassment — [Valence trust and security](https://www.valence.co/trust-and-security), [Valence FAQ](https://support.valence.co/en/articles/9100961-valence-s-ai-coach-nadia-introduction-and-faqs)
- Valence does not train its own foundation models and prohibits AI partners from training on user data — [Valence trust and security](https://www.valence.co/trust-and-security)
- Hybrid players (BetterUp, CoachHub, Torch) route to human coaches when needed — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250121963707/en/BetterUp-Launches-AICoaching-Bridging-Human-Expertise-and-AI), [Risely](https://risely.me/compare/best-ai-coaching-platforms/)
- Rocky.ai lacks compliance certifications per one competitor-authored review — [Risely](https://risely.me/compare/best-ai-coaching-platforms/)

### Inferences
- Human escalation and enterprise-contract privacy terms serve as the practical safety boundary; no explicit "not a licensed therapist" disclaimer was found in the retrieved material, though it likely exists in terms of service.

### Gaps
- Explicit not-therapy disclaimers, crisis-handling protocols, EU AI Act (employment-related AI is high-risk territory) positioning: not found.

## Pricing (B2C and B2B)

### Takeaway
Consumer/self-serve tools run roughly $0 to $20 per month; enterprise is opaque and quote-based, with BetterUp estimated in the thousands per user per year.

### Cited Findings
- Rocky.ai: free tier; individual from $9.99/month (another review says ~$12.99); teams quoted at $10/user/month or $19.90/seat; Stork lists business plans from $29 and Silver at $199/month for 20 seats; sources conflict — [AI Tools Bakery](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/rocky-ai-review/), [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/en/rocky-ai). LearnWorlds listed $199/month in Dec 2024 [OLD >12m].

### Inferences
- AI-only enterprise seats are likely far cheaper than human coaching, but no source verified this.
