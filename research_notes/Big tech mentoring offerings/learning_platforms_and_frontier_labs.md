# Learning platforms, skills assessment and frontier-lab "de facto mentors" (as of 15 Oct 2026)

Method caveat: about 17 web searches; no full pages were fetched, so everything below rests on search-result summaries (several of them secondary or vendor-authored). Not repeated here: items already in the existing landscape report (Valence, BetterUp, CoachHub, Torch, Ezra, Rocky.ai, ADPList, MentorCruise, Pluralsight Iris basics, DataCamp ~$28/mo, Sana/Workday, the de Haan/Terblanche/Nowack head-to-head, Science sycophancy study). Prices in GBP were not found for any product; flagged as gaps.

## Learning and skills platforms not yet (or only thinly) covered

### Takeaway
Most skills platforms bolt a catalogue-bound AI tutor or role-play tool onto content, and sell the richest versions to employers. Consolidation (Coursera + Udemy) and enterprise add-on pricing mean few of these are individual-facing mentors. Consumer price points found: Khanmigo $4/mo, Codecademy and Boot.dev ~$20-60/mo (conflicting sources).

## Frontier-lab consumer features as de facto mentors

### Takeaway
All three labs now ship study/learning modes and cross-chat memory, ChatGPT adds proactive Pulse, but none was found with an explicit editable goal model, scheduled follow-up check-ins on user goals, or skill-level tracking.

### Cited Findings
- ChatGPT study mode is available on all plans; it uses memory for personalisation when on, and works with memory off. — [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/articles/11780217)
- ChatGPT Pulse launched in preview 25 Sept 2025 for Pro ($200/mo US) on mobile, proactive daily updates drawn from chats, with Plus/free expansion promised; I found no confirmed 2026 availability. — [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/a-i/openai-a-i/327299/openai-launches-chatgpt-pulse-in-preview); [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/artificial-intelligence/chatgpt-pulse-price-subscription-explained/)
- Claude: memory extended to Pro and Max on 23 Oct 2025, with per-project memory, view/edit controls and incognito chats; Pro $20/mo ($17 annual); sources conflict on Projects availability on the free plan. Learning mode described as asking questions rather than answering (low-quality source). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory); [rgb.ir](https://rgb.ir/en/ai/claude/)
- Gemini Guided Learning launched Aug 2025 (step-by-step, comprehension checks, visuals); Google AI Pro $19.99/mo US, EUR 21.99 France; no source on whether Guided Learning/Gems use memory; the free AI Pro year for students excluded the UK at launch. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/new-gemini-tools-students-august-2025/)

### Inferences
- "Where they stop": no source shows structured goals, skill ratings, or user-scheduled accountability loops in any of the three. This is absence of evidence; the labs ship monthly.

### Gaps
- No 2026 release notes read; GBP prices, Plus-tier Pulse status, Gemini memory scope, Claude learning mode primary docs all unverified.

## UK context

### Takeaway
UK policy is employer- and levy-centred: short "apprenticeship units" and free government AI training shape what employers buy and what individuals can get free, which pressures price for a paid individual product.

### Cited Findings
- Growth and Skills Levy: apprenticeship units (30-140 delivery hours, 1-16 weeks, ages 19+, already employed) from April 2026, first wave including AI leadership. Provider blogs claim more (50% levy on short courses, 12-month fund expiry); unconfirmed against gov.uk. — [Apprenticeship help centre](https://help.apprenticeships.education.gov.uk/hc/en-gb/articles/33997136206226-Apprenticeship-units); [Pareto](https://www.pareto.co.uk/resources/blogs/the-growth-and-skills-levy-2026-a-strategic-guide-for-uk-employers)
- Jan 2026: government targets free AI skills training for 10 million workers by 2030 (up from 7.5M), via AI Skills Hub with short courses and a government-backed AI foundations badge; partners include Accenture, Amazon, Google, Microsoft, IBM, NHS, techUK, CBI, FSB; part of TechFirst (£187M), £27M TechLocal. — [gov.uk](https://gov.uk/government/news/free-ai-training-for-all-as-government-and-industry-programme-expands-to-provide-10-million-workers-with-key-ai-skills-by-2030); [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/government/govt-plan-ai-training-10-million-2030)
- Multiverse publishes employer guidance on the levy changes. — [Multiverse](https://www.multiverse.io/blog/labour-growth-and-skills-levy-what-it-means-for-employers)

### Inferences
- Free foundation-level AI training sets a floor; an individual product must target beyond basics (career navigation, accountability). Levy money flows to employers, not individuals.

### Gaps
- BCS role not found; data/AI apprenticeship standards, UK AI skills framework and Skills England approved-course list not reviewed; Multiverse UK-specific current offer unverified.

## Efficacy evidence (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Guardrailed AI tutors show learning gains in trials; unguarded chatbots can harm unassisted performance; AI coaching shows short-term goal gains mostly via accountability, weaker than humans.

### Cited Findings
- Schimpf, Voigt, Bohne (preprint, Mar 2026, arXiv 2603.17887): preregistered RCT, N=517, LLM career coach vs written-reflection questionnaire vs control; coach beat control on goal progress at two weeks (d=0.33, p=.016) but not the questionnaire; perceived accountability mediated. Not peer reviewed; two-week horizon. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17887)
- de Haan, Terblanche, Nowack (2026, HRDI), 114 senior leaders: effects only for human coaching (already in existing report; DOI 10.1080/13678868.2026.2633990 newly noted). — [summary via search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17887)
- Bastani et al., PNAS vol 122 (July 2025), ~1,000 high-school maths students: unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores but cut later exam scores 17% vs control; guardrailed tutor avoided the loss. Abstract-level reading only. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html); [Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)
- UK secondary maths exploratory RCT, N=165: AI tutoring similar to expert human tutoring. — [arXiv 2512.23633](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23633v1)
- IZA DP 18338, 334 university students: AI tutor +0.23 SD on test scores. — [IZA](https://www.iza.org/publications/dp18338/1/citations/ris)
- Harvard physics RCT (Scientific Reports 2025): AI tutor beat in-class active learning (effect sizes not confirmed). Medical education reviews: mixed, low certainty (secondary blog). — [Harvard SERL](https://serl.fas.harvard.edu/publication/ai-tutoring-outperforms-class-active-learning-rct-introducing-novel-research-based); [iatrox](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/do-ai-tutors-improve-medical-education-evidence)

### Inferences
- Accountability and structure appear to be the active ingredients; design must withhold answers to avoid skill erosion. Evidence is mostly students, not working data/AI professionals.

### Gaps
- No trial of an LLM mentor with memory on working technical professionals; no long-term retention data; full texts unread.
